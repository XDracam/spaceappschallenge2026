## 1115m-r01 — rework of R1115m-r01-03

**Problem.** Science had nothing to do in the weather mission: all nine needs were inside the starting research, research took 4-5 months against a launch after 6, and Science could only learn of a need after the Planner had ordered and Procurement had pressed "Ask science to develop it". Evidence: player 3 made 0 game actions and waited 635 of 720 s (events.jsonl), asked four times which research the design needs and was told "none" (chat.jsonl), and noted "Unclear whether any research can pay off before launch" (notes.jsonl t=424); `state/00959.json` has no research project and no request.

**What changed.**

1. Mission content (weather satellite only): the agency's starting research is lower for two parts of the starting design. Camera limit 6 -> 4 and the imagery goal now needs performance 4.5 (was 3; the design's camera is 4.5), antenna limit 3000 -> 200 kbps (the design's antenna is 600; the camera sends 225). The catalogue cannot sell either part until Science has researched it. One first-step tech covers each (Larger sensor arrays or Adaptive optics; Ka-band downlink or Larger deployable dish). The existing Comms project "Northgate imager research" is a second way to the camera.
2. Research time per mission: `MissionDef.researchTimeFactor` (weather 0.5, Mars unchanged). A first-step tech now takes 2-2.5 months in the weather mission, so a project started in the first minute ends in March and leaves the 3 months the fastest camera supplier needs before the launch on 30 Jun.
3. Science reads the needs from the Planner's rocket instead of waiting for a request. Every part of the design that is beyond research and not under contract appears under "The mission needs" from the first second, follows the Planner's sliders, and names the day research has to be finished for the part to arrive before launch. The request step is deleted: `research.requests`, Procurement's "Ask science to develop it" / "Withdraw" and the "Buy or develop" block are gone (state version 4).
4. Procurement sees how far Science is, on the need itself: the "Beyond research" chip now reads "Beyond research · Science not started" or "· Science until 17 Mar", the need also gets its "Order by" date while it waits for research, and the inspector says which limit is missing and until when Science is on it. When the research succeeds, the news line says which need can now be bought and the chip disappears.

**Why this approach.** The cause was three hand-overs in a row (Planner orders, Procurement asks, Science acts) in front of a mission that needed no research. Deleting the request removes two of them and lets the design itself carry the information; lowering two limits gives Science a job the mission depends on without a new system. The buy-or-develop choice of D-020 stays as wait for Science or open a Call, decided by talking rather than by a button.

**Added to the screens.** Science: panel "The mission needs" (replaces "Requests from Procurement") with a "Finish by <date>" line and the shortest routes with cost and duration; the end date in the tech inspector ("2 months, done 11 Mar 2030"). Procurement: Science's state in the existing chip and one callout.
**Removed from the screens.** Science: "Requests from Procurement"; ★ marks on techs deeper than the shortest route. Procurement: the "Buy or develop" block with its two paragraphs and the "Ask science to develop it" / "Withdraw" buttons; the static callout "Created beyond research: <keys>…"; the two request news lines.

**What it could break.** The weather mission is harder: both researches succeed together 81 % of the time; a failed one costs two more months and then needs priority delivery, a Call or a later launch. A launch date that is not moved still scrubs (R1115m-r01-02 is open). Science still has little to do once both projects run (about 2 of the 6 minutes). Mars games: Procurement can no longer file a request; Science sees the needs directly. Saves of version 3 are migrated (requests dropped). `Task.beyondResearch` is still written but nothing reads it any more.

**For the team to look at.** Whether dropping the explicit request (D-020: "resource planning decides buy or develop") is acceptable; whether 4 / 4.5 for the camera and 200 kbps for the antenna are the right numbers; whether research should give more than access to parts (reliability, lead time), so Science has decisions in the second half. ## 1115d-r01 — rework of R1115d-r01-01

**Problem.** Nobody could see into or change the launch odds: 92 % / 86 % was fixed by the first nine orders, the suggestion picked the cheapest offer, no research or Comms project touched reliability, and 234 M and the last 100 game days had no use. Evidence: all four stations look for a lever and find none (chat.jsonl n36-n45), "my screen only shows the totals, so I can't tell you" (n40), Planner idle from t=268, Science from t=255, Procurement from t=530 until the launch at t=950 (events.jsonl); the booster engine (97.5 %) then failed and the result was 0 of 2 goals with 135.6 M of 370 M spent (summary.json).

**What changed.**

1. Science can test delivered parts. A free research team tests one delivered part of the Planner's design for 2 months times the mission's `researchTimeFactor` (1 month in the weather mission) at 20 % of the part's contract price (at least 1 M). The test always works and halves the part's risk of failure (97.5 % becomes 98.8 %). One test per part. It counts only if it ends before the launch day. A test is a research project with a `partId`: it takes one of the three team slots, can be cancelled like research, and follows Comms' research pace.
2. Science's left column has a third section, "Part tests": delivered parts as cards with one button ("Test · 7.1 M · 1 mo → 98.2 %"), weakest first; parts under contract in one compact card "On their way" with reliability and arrival date, in red when a test would no longer fit before the launch, with the line "Procurement can speed up a delivery, the Planner can move the launch."
3. The Planner's Goals card names the parts behind the percentages: "Weakest parts (Science can test delivered parts): Orbital camera 97% · Booster engine 97.5% · Upper stage engine 98.2% tested". A click shows the part in the Rocket panel. The numbers are the factors the goal chance is multiplied from (`GoalResult.factors`).
4. Procurement: an offer row reads "96.5 % reliable" instead of a bare "96.5 %" and adds "time to test" when the part would arrive early enough for a test before launch; "All contracts" has a Reliability column that shows "98.2 % · tested by Science"; the suggestion ranks fit, delivery, budget, then the lightest part and its most reliable supplier (was: cheapest).

**Why this approach.** The cause was that reliability was decided once, by default, in the first two minutes, and nothing afterwards could act on it. A test needs the delivered part, a lab team and a month before launch, so the waiting half of the mission becomes three connected decisions: Procurement chooses between the more reliable supplier and the faster one whose part can still be tested (or pays for priority delivery), the Planner sees the weakest parts and may move the launch to fit more tests (which Comms then has to explain to the Weather Service, an event that already exists), and Science decides which parts get its three teams. It reuses the lab slots, the project list, the budget and the launch date; the only new concept is "tested". The alternative in the finding (existing techs also raise reliability) would have needed authored numbers per tech and would have stayed inside Science.

**Added to the screens.** Science: section "Part tests" (one sentence, cards for delivered parts with a Test button, one card "On their way", two count lines). Planner: one line "Weakest parts …" in the Goals card. Procurement: the word "reliable" and the conditional "time to test" on offer rows, the Reliability column, "tested by Science" on a tested contract. News line on every finished test ("Science tested the Upper stage engine (Lumen HL-120): reliability 96.5 % → 98.2 %.").
**Removed from the screens.** Planner: the hint "from part reliability" and its hover-only explanation. Procurement: the bare unlabelled percentage on offer rows; "then price" in the suggestion note. Result card: finished part tests are not counted as "research successes".

**What it could break.**
- Balance. With every part tested the weather mission goes from about 92 % / 86 % to about 96 % / 93 %; all nine tests cost about 20 M, which is little against the unused budget. Time and teams are the real limit: with the orders of round 1115d-r01 only five of nine parts arrive a month before launch, and the three weakest (camera, upper-stage engine, apogee engine) do not.
- The suggestion no longer picks the cheapest offer. The nine parts cost more (Helix is 1.38x), and the more reliable suppliers are slower, so by default fewer parts can be tested; that is the trade-off Procurement now has, but a player who only clicks "Review suggestion" does not meet it. A first version ranked reliability before part size and suggested a 260 t tank for an 80 t need, which left the rocket too weak to lift off; "lightest part" comes first for that reason.
- A test that is still running at launch is lost without refund (the screens warn before and after starting it). A test is also lost if the part's contract is cancelled meanwhile.
- State version 5 (two optional fields: `Contract.tested`, `ResearchProject.partId`); version 4 saves load unchanged.

**For the team to look at.** Whether halving the risk for 20 % of the price and one month is the right size, and whether one test per part is enough; whether a test should be able to find a fault (repair time) instead of always helping; whether the suggestion should prefer the faster supplier when its part can be tested (it does not); whether the Planner needs a hint how many tests a later launch date would allow. Not checked: four players, a full flight with tested parts, the Mars mission (2-month tests, engine clusters), priority delivery amendments as the way to make a part testable. Drop it with `git revert <hash>`.
