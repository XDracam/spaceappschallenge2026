The supplied 17-page copy carries a 2013 copyright. The challenge links a [JPL repository record](https://dataverse.jpl.nasa.gov/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=hdl:2014/44299). The paper describes early mission concept development, especially CMLs 1–4. Its claims concern engineering practice. The gameplay applications below are assistant proposals, with enjoyment and multiplayer pacing still untested. Its historical procedural references would require current-source checks before use as present-day NASA requirements.

## Concrete scenario proposal

### Role-boundary clarification after user review

The user questioned the overlap with comms' opportunities and mission planning's approaches. The role mapping above needs this qualification. Comms' documented user direction covers industry relationships, partner negotiations and opportunities, including parts delivered as Offers to procurement. Planning owns mission architecture and technical configuration. Science evaluates scientific value and research methods. Procurement compares available supply, runs Calls, signs and manages Contracts, and reports acquisition cost, timing and contractual consequences. Make-or-buy and testing investment span several roles. Acquisition of a test service belongs with procurement, while the technical team defines the test and interprets its result.

Real procurement also includes market research and commercial supplier negotiation. Our game distributes some of that work to comms. This is a deliberate game-role allocation supported by the comms specification. The CML paper describes work across a mission team and does not establish the ownership of our four stations. Managing the entire shared Budget and allocating resources to departments are game-role choices beyond the contracting function alone.

Checked November 14, 2026 against [NASA NPR 7123.1D, Chapter 4](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=Chapter4). Sections 4.2–4.4 assign technical requirements, proposal evaluation participation, technical monitoring and acceptance participation to the technical team, coordinated with the contracting officer. See also the game's [comms specification](../../communications/README.md#role) and [role actions](../../procurement/role-actions.md).

An authored instrument option offers more scientific return but increases power and data demands. Procurement independently finds a proven lower-capability instrument, a development route using science's time and testing funds, and a partner-supplied route whose access comms must investigate. Planning evaluates the mass, power and schedule implications. Science evaluates which observations each route retains. Procurement compares the total acquisition and service costs and the remaining reserve. Players discuss and choose their approach through their own conversation arrangements.

With the clarification above, procurement's independent discovery in this example is limited to comparing supply already accessible through its catalogue and Calls. Comms pursues partner opportunities. Science assesses the development route. Planning evaluates and selects the technical mission approach with the team.

These are alternative directions for exploration. Quantities, prices, supplier terms, development effects, information visibility and phase pacing remain unspecified. No scenario was implemented or played for this review. Role allocation alone would establish a resource destination. Additional consequential sourcing and investment choices would be needed to investigate the reported gameplay weakness.
