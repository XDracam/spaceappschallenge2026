# Procurement order options

## User input

> also we could just, simply, parametrize our orders? longer timespans leading to differing success rates and costs in different ways. plus some of these. Give me a list of things to add that you think make sense and provide overall good and interesting gameplay, ideally with good interactions that don't bloat other roles too much

Context from the conversation includes comms owning relationships and negotiated opportunities, planning owning mission approaches, and players arranging their own voice or in-person conversation. The user previously requested precise attribution to the CML paper. The following mechanics are assistant proposals responding to this new design request. Their effects and entertainment value have not been validated.

## Proposed additions, in priority order

1. **Configurable orders.** Choose delivery date and assurance level. Preview total cost and the specific affected probability. Extra schedule allowance can reduce rush costs, while extra qualification can cost more and improve operational reliability. Give each authored supplier a small set of distinct response curves, limits and diminishing returns. Keep probability of timely delivery separate from operational reliability if both are modeled. Initially prefer one risk type per option and preserve the existing operational reliability meaning.
2. **Contract amendments.** Let procurement price an acceleration, extension or added qualification on an active Contract, with benefits limited by work already completed. Show the new arrival, cost and reliability before committing. This provides ongoing decisions as requirements and funding change. The original agreement stays valid until an amendment is accepted.
3. **Early reservations.** For selected long-lead items, pay to hold a production slot and confirm the final specification by a stated deadline within stated limits. Compare the reservation fee with committing fully or waiting. Procurement discusses confidence in the requirement with planning. Supplier capacity need only be represented by a few authored opportunities.
4. **Calls with a chosen response deadline.** A quick Call reaches a limited set of authored responders, while a longer window can include additional suppliers. Show which responses are possible and when. Avoid allowing repeated Calls to reroll better deals. Comms continues to control relationship-based access and special terms.
5. **Purchases usable by departments.** Let procurement acquire equipment or services for Science and other departments without a Task. Receiving stations use them through their existing project flows. Initially pick a small number of resources with explicit effects. Ownership, simultaneous use and project reservation rules need agreement before implementation.
6. **Partial deliveries and shared capacity.** Where a need is divisible, compare a small early shipment and a later balance against one full shipment. For an initial example, deliver a research test unit early and flight units later. Quantities and contract records remain centralized in procurement. Receiving stations show when the relevant resources are usable.
7. **Recovery decisions with usable lead time.** A small set of authored supplier problems can offer priced choices such as accepting a delay, paying for recovery or switching to a known alternative. Present options while a response can still matter. Procurement handles contractual recovery, planning decides whether to change the mission schedule, and comms can pursue its existing relationship actions.

## Suggested first experiment

Start with configurable orders, amendments and department use. These build on existing Offers, delivery dates, reliability, cancellation and shared state, while addressing the incomplete route from spoken research needs to usable purchases. Add reservations and more elaborate delivery structures if this first set produces worthwhile decisions and leaves a pacing gap.

An optional acquisition draft and a remaining-budget preview could help compare the effect of these choices across needs. These are supporting interface features already explored in I-050 and I-064.

No numerical balance, success guarantee, new role responsibility or implementation is selected here. An authored multiplayer scenario must establish whether choices remain distinct, whether participants can understand the consequences, and whether procurement has consequential work while deliveries are pending.
