# Prototype exploration

**Status:** historical first-experiment proposals, drafted November 14, 2026. The game is now playable; see [current prototype](current-prototype.md) and [evidence availability](evidence-availability.md). The proposals below are retained as exploration, not the implementation plan.

## Central question

Can multiplayer mission planning make communication fun while keeping every role meaningfully active at the same time, without hard waits? How do players' decisions affect one another in ways they can understand and respond to?

## Candidate experiments

These are options to compare, not an ordered or approved implementation plan:

1. **Concurrent role exercise:** give several people simultaneous, distinct work and a shared mission goal; observe idle time, communication, and conflicting priorities.
2. **Communications event exercise:** present messages and player-triggered events, explore response options, and observe whether consequences reach procurement, science, or planning in understandable ways.
3. **Mission flow exercise:** explore how broad destination goals, planning, real-time events, and the earlier one-shot simulation idea might fit together.
4. **Progression exercise:** compare a roguelite run with a campaign that retains some overarching progress. Explore failure without deciding the structure in advance.
5. **Shared task exercise:** start from a broad goal, uncover tasks, compare catalogue and Call options, and observe what happens when a constraint or schedule changes. Explicitly probe whether a solution remains feasible and every role remains engaged.
6. **Personnel comparison:** explore how named people, skilled pools, or simple headcounts communicate training and create engaging choices across roles.

A paper exercise and a shared planning sheet were earlier assistant suggestions (I-006 and I-008). They could help with some questions; the right format for real-time multiplayer remains open. Prototype mechanics and invented values may be chosen provisionally for an experiment without making them final design decisions.

## What to observe

- Are all roles active at once, or does someone wait for a dependency?
- Is the communication itself enjoyable and useful?
- Can one role's choices create understandable work or trade-offs for another?
- Does handling a message produce meaningful choices and visible results?
- Does switching roles help, and what happens to work in progress?
- Are the science and planning activities engaging?
- Which surprises feel interesting or arbitrary?
- Does failure support the intended experience and the chosen progression experiment?

Record actual observations, limitations, and proposed revisions using the [playtest template](playtests/TEMPLATE.md). At the time these proposals were drafted, no observations had been recorded. Later dated records are linked from the [history index](history/README.md).
