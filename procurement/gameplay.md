# Catalogue and Budget gameplay

Current direction, November 14, 2026, following the user's correction of the second mockup. The user describes procurement as fulfilling other roles' needs and completing shared Tasks by shopping from a catalogue and managing the available Budget. Interface layout and the example session below are assistant proposals.

## Player responsibility

The November 14 follow-up selects amendments to Contracts and configurable Call response deadlines with visible consequences. Science resource needs are communicated by players and supplied directly for department use without Science-created procurement Tasks. Different delivery quantities and dates use separate orders. Production-slot reservations remain optional and bespoke supplier recovery events are deferred. The detailed order parameters are selected as a prototype target in [D-018](../docs/decisions.md#d-018--procurement-order-and-department-supply-prototype); the assurance control remains unselected. See [D-017](../docs/decisions.md#d-017--procurement-amendments-call-timing-and-direct-department-needs) and the [source feedback](../docs/notes/2026-11-14-procurement-order-decisions.md). Implementation progress and verification are recorded separately.

The procurement player manages the available Budget across needs arising throughout the mission preparation. They find and acquire things other roles need, including research requisites, personnel and mission hardware. Choices about what to buy, when and for whom implicitly determine how funds are distributed. Other stations, including communications, can change overall funding.

Tasks remain shared goals. Players discuss needs, priorities, alternatives and compromises directly. Procurement consumes the persistent shared needs supplied by the other systems. Requests for advice or help can remain spoken conversation under D-007 and D-008.

## Sustained gameplay

- Understand the current needs across the team and the funds already committed.
- Browse and search the authored catalogue for equipment, parts, personnel and services. Compare relevant properties, qualifications, prices, lead times and reliability where applicable.
- Discover useful options and bring possibilities or constraints to the people who understand their implications.
- Decide what to acquire now and how much Budget to retain for other known or emerging needs.
- Acquire through Contracts and connect supplied quantities to the needs they address. The Contract list remains inventory.
- Track what is committed, due and usable. Reconsider outstanding purchases when priorities, funding, research or supplier conditions change.
- Open a Call when it offers a useful additional route, with responses drawn from authored content.

The primary activity is the ongoing acquisition and Budget management across this changing collection of needs. The previous mockups foregrounded a few deliberately contrasted Offers and an engine-versus-camera dilemma. Those examples can occur within play, but they were too narrow to define the station's gameplay.

## Example session to explore

Begin with several simultaneous needs and an authored catalogue. Quantities and prices await balancing. The examples describe potential play, rather than selected mission or personnel rules.

1. Science says a research project needs laboratory equipment and a qualified researcher. Planning has a propulsion need and an upcoming need for a power system. Comms is exploring a relationship activity that might also require resources.
2. Procurement browses personnel and equipment. It finds a researcher with the relevant qualifications and several equipment options, then discusses which purchase would let science make useful progress soonest.
3. The player commits part of the Budget to the resulting acquisition and checks the remaining funds against the other needs. Planning's expensive component can be procured now, deferred while pursuing another route, or discussed as a constraint on the rest of the plan.
4. Procurement finds an alternative power solution while browsing. Its timing, capability or cost could help the team. The player explains the discovery to planning, which evaluates its mission consequences.
5. A comms outcome changes funding. The procurement player revisits what can now be afforded, or what must be delayed, with the other players.
6. Science completes research that changes catalogue options or prices. More needs may emerge from its next project. The procurement player updates the acquisition plan while outstanding Contracts continue to progress.
7. Approaching launch, the player checks coverage and delivery gaps and discusses what the team can still solve with the remaining Budget and time.

The interesting decisions emerge from these needs, discoveries and commitments interacting. The player should be able to find several workable acquisition plans and explain why they favored one. Exact staffing, assignment and training mechanics remain open.

## Proposed full-screen layout

Target at least a 1920 × 1080 desktop. The user rejected the cramped chat-window presentation.

- Give most of the screen to the catalogue, with several visible items, useful categories, search and comparison detail.
- Keep needs across the team visible beside it, including criteria, timing and what is already supplied or promised.
- Keep the Budget, outstanding commitments and calendar visible while shopping.
- Provide accessible Contract and Call views for tracking acquisitions and investigating supply options.
- Let the player move between categories and needs without being locked into one Task's preselected Offers.

Specific catalogue layout, controls and the presentation of buying and Allocation need another design pass. Earlier proposals for combined Contract and Allocation confirmation can still be considered.

## Validation scenarios

### Research procurement alongside mission hardware

Science needs staff and laboratory equipment while planning needs a costly part. Observe whether the player can discover purchases, understand affordability and discuss priority with both roles. The resulting acquisition mix should show how the player distributes Budget across the mission.

### Discovering a useful alternative

While looking for one thing, the player finds an authored resource that could help another need. Observe whether catalogue browsing produces discovery and worthwhile conversation, and whether players can judge the alternative without prior specialist training.

### Funding changes during ongoing acquisition

Comms changes the available funding while several needs and commitments exist. Observe whether the player can revise what to buy or defer, and understand existing obligations. Actual changes to funds follow the authored outcome.

### New research changes what is worth buying

Science unlocks a cheaper or more suitable option while other Contracts are active. Observe whether the player can understand new possibilities, preserve useful commitments and talk through replacement or new purchases.

### Several needs remain at the preparation deadline

Some needs are covered and others have gaps. Observe whether the player can quickly identify remaining useful actions and explain the Budget and schedule constraints to the team. Detailed mission physics remain within planning's scope.

The desired experience is fun, accessible resource acquisition and coordination. None of these scenarios has yet been played. Earlier [gameplay vignettes](workflows.md), [state checks](state-walkthroughs.md), and [interfaces](interfaces.md) provide supporting detail.
