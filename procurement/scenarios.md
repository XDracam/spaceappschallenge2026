# Procurement gameplay scenarios

Assistant brainstorming examples requested by the user on November 14, 2026. These situations are candidates for design walkthroughs. Outcomes depend on player choices and the rules eventually selected.

## 1. The cheap engine misses the window

Planning needs an engine for the next launch opportunity. One supplier is cheap but late, another is fast but unreliable, and the reliable fast option consumes most of the budget. Procurement asks whether planning can move the launch or accept a riskier option.

Check whether offers expose the tradeoffs and planning receives a useful forecast before a contract is signed.

## 2. Science and planning compete for the last funds

Science requests a better instrument while planning requests a backup engine. Either purchase fits the remaining budget, but both together exceed it. Both players argue that their request matters more.

Check who sets priorities and whether competing commitments are visible before money is promised twice.

## 3. The mission changes after signing

Procurement has contracted an engine. Planning adds a payload and now needs more thrust. The old contract still exists, but its engine no longer meets the revised need. Procurement can seek a replacement, pay cancellation costs, or ask planning to reconsider the payload.

Check task revisions, reassessment, contract history, and what happens to the old allocation.

## 4. The part arrives before launch, but too late for assembly

A supplier slips delivery by a week. The part will arrive before launch, but planning needs it earlier for assembly and checks. Procurement investigates expedited delivery or an alternative component.

Check the meaning of the need-by date and the distinction between delivery and mission readiness.

## 5. One bulk deal serves several departments

A supplier offers three computers as a discounted package. Planning needs two for flight hardware and science needs one for a laboratory project. Later, planning asks for all three.

Check quantity allocation, competing reservations, and the consequences of reassigning a contracted unit.

## 6. The objective disappears after delivery

A sensor is already delivered when its science objective is dropped. Another task might use it, or procurement might pursue a return if its terms permit one. The original contract and its spending remain relevant.

Check how unused, reassigned, cancelled, and historical commitments appear without losing their cost or provenance.

## 7. Funding falls while contracts are active

A political dispute cuts agency funding after procurement has signed several contracts. Upcoming payments may exceed the funds left. Procurement asks comms for help while evaluating which commitments it can afford to cancel.

Check unpaid obligations, cancellation settlements, negative available funds, and the conversation about funding and the changes the team actually makes.

## 8. Industry relations produces an attractive condition

Comms negotiates a discounted launch service if the mission also carries a sponsor's payload. Procurement sees a better offer while planning must decide whether the extra payload is feasible.

Check how a conditional opportunity connects an offer to several tasks and whether the team can understand its full consequences before accepting it. Conditional offers are an additional possibility to evaluate.

## 9. Research makes an existing purchase less attractive

Science unlocks a lighter instrument after procurement has contracted a heavier one. The lighter model could free enough capacity for another objective, but switching costs money and takes time.

Check access changes, replacement decisions, and communication of the improvement and switching costs to planning.

## 10. A Call returns an imperfect custom solution

The catalogue has no engine satisfying planning's mass and thrust limits. An authored Call offers a custom design with a long lead time and a second design that needs a relaxed mass limit. Procurement brings both options to planning.

Check how offers can address a task partially and how the team negotiates constraints. This scenario leaves the intended solution guarantee under Q-022 open.

## 11. The astronaut can arrive before they can qualify

Planning requests a pilot qualified for a particular vehicle. A qualified candidate is expensive and available late. A cheaper candidate is available immediately but needs training from staff already committed to science work.

Check capability, availability, training dependencies, and whether the personnel model can represent the staff conflict. Its rules remain open.

## 12. Two parts need the same testing service

Two components require a specialist test facility before assembly. Procurement secures a facility slot, but both departments expect to use it at the same time. Another slot costs more and may force rescheduling.

Check whether service contracts need start/end times and capacity reservations. The current quantity-only allocation proposal cannot express this timing conflict fully.
